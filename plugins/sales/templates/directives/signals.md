# 📡 Buying signals

> **A signal is a public, dated event that says: this company probably needs you now.**
> It tells you *who* to contact and *when*, and it gives you the first line of the message.

Every business has its own signals. Build yours with the **signal-builder** skill: tell
Claude what usually happens at a company just before it buys from you, and it writes a
spec in `directives/signals/<name>.md` and tests it on a small batch.

## 💡 Ideas to start from

| Signal | The logic | Typical source |
|---|---|---|
| Hiring a specific role | The job ad names the pain | LinkedIn Jobs, job boards, careers pages |
| New leader in a role | New leaders change suppliers and tools in their first 90 days | Sales Navigator "changed jobs" |
| Expansion | A new site, market or product line means new work | Company news page, press, LinkedIn posts |
| Event presence | Exhibiting or speaking: active, has budget, shared context | Exhibitor and speaker lists |
| Tool or stack change | They adopt or drop a tool you complement or replace | Job ads naming tools, tech-detection sites |
| Regulation or deadline | A rule forces a change by a date | Industry news, associations |
| Funding or fast growth | New money or fast hiring means new budget | Press, registries, LinkedIn headcount |
| Warm path | A 1st or 2nd-degree link, or a mutual contact | Your LinkedIn network |

> **Tip:** test a new signal on a small batch, write the yield in Learnings
> (e.g. "50 ads → 3 fits"), and stop signals that only bring noise.
